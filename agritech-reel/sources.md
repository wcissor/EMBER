## H1 · Indoor farms

> "Investors poured billions into indoor farms. Most of them went bust."
> On screen: "Nearly $1B raised. Bankrupt."

**Status: change proposed.** The numbers hold, but "most of them" overstates the source, and Plenty did not stay bankrupt.

- **Source:** TechCrunch, Mary Ann Azevedo, 24 Mar 2025:
  https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/vertical-farming-company-plenty-files-for-bankruptcy-after-raising-nearly-1b
  - "Vertical farming company Plenty has filed for bankruptcy, the company said in a press release on Monday."
  - "Since its 2014 inception, South San Francisco-based Plenty has raised nearly $1 billion in funding from a variety of investors, including SoftBank Investment Advisers, Walmart, Bezos Expeditions, and Jeff Bezos as an angel investor."
  - "In November 2024, agtech unicorn Bowery Farming was reported to be shutting down after raising more than $700 million in funding … And in 2023, AeroFarms and AppHarvest filed for bankruptcy protection."
  - "The industry has been hard hit by bankruptcies in recent years."
- **Later:** Plenty completed its restructuring and emerged from Chapter 11 on 29 May 2025, focused on strawberries:
  https://www.plenty.ag/plenty-completes-restructuring-emerges-from-chapter-11/ (via search results; the page was not opened).

  - Source tag: "SOURCE: TECHCRUNCH 2025 · PLENTY". The tag names the company, so the figure isn't read as belonging to the farm in the stock footage.
  - The posting captions change the same way ("Many of the biggest went bust" / "ən böyüklərinin bir çoxu iflas etdi").

## H3 · Cows without fences

> "In New Zealand, some cows don't need fences anymore."
> On screen: a dotted fence line drawn on a real pasture

**Status: holds, as a hook.**

- Halter's home page (https://www.halterhq.com):
  - "Virtually fence, move and monitor cows…"
  - "Set break fences without reels and standards"
- The Australian Financial Review's headline (23 Jun 2025, cited by Wikipedia's Halter article) was "Smart collar for cows could abolish the need for farm fences".
- Halter's pages describe virtual fences replacing the movable fences inside a farm. They don't say farms remove their outer fences.
  - The hook simplifies this.
  - B4 and B4s ("The fence is an app") make it exact.

## B4, B4s, H3 · Collars instead of fences

> On screen: no number

**Status: mostly holds.** Solar collars, sound and vibration cues and app-set fences are all confirmed. The exact split ("a beep turns them, a buzz moves them") is not stated by Halter.

- **Halter, How it works:** https://www.halterhq.com/how-it-works
  - "a solar-powered, ergonomically designed smart collar"
  - "Directional audio and vibration cues to guide animals in a way that is predictable and reliable."
  - "Farmers set a shift in their Halter app, and minutes later, animals move in the real world."
- **TechCrunch, 4 Apr 2026:** https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/04/unpacking-peter-thiels-big-bet-on-solar-powered-cow-collars/
  - "Cattle are trained to respond to audio and vibration cues from the collar … 'Then you're able to guide them and shift them around on sound and vibration alone.'"
  - "Halter's collar is on more than a million cattle across more than 2,000 farms in New Zealand, Australia, and the United States."
- **Wikipedia, Halter (company):** "Halter is a New Zealand agricultural technology company that develops solar-powered cattle collars and farm management software for virtual fencing…"

  - B4 EN: "In New Zealand, cows wear solar collars. Sound and vibration steer them. The fence is an app."
  - B4 AZ: "Yeni Zelandiyada inəklər günəş enerjili boyunluq taxır. Səs və titrəmə onları yönləndirir. Hasar isə telefondakı tətbiqdir."

## B6 · Water

> "Why now? Farming uses about seventy percent of the world's fresh water."
> On screen: "≈70% of fresh water"

**Status: the number holds; a small wording change is proposed.** The 70% is a share of the fresh water people take from rivers and aquifers ("withdrawals"), not of all the fresh water on Earth.

- **Proposed lines:**
  - EN: "Why now? About seventy percent of the fresh water we use goes to farming."
  - AZ: "Niyə məhz indi? İstifadə etdiyimiz şirin suyun təxminən 70%-i kənd təsərrüfatına gedir."
  - On screen: "≈70%" with "of the fresh water we use".
- **FAO newsroom, 12 Dec 2025, on the 2025 AQUASTAT Water Data Snapshot:**
  https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/renewable-water-availability-per-person-plunges-7-percent-in-a-decade-as-global-scarcity-deepens--fao-data-shows/en
  - "Agriculture remains the largest water-using sector globally, accounting for 72 percent of withdrawals in many regions."
- **Smart Water Magazine, on the same Snapshot (2022 data):**
  https://smartwatermagazine.com/news/smart-water-magazine/global-water-under-pressure-what-aquastat-2025-reveals-about-agriculture
  - Reports agriculture at 71 per cent of total freshwater withdrawals globally in 2022, with 15 per cent for industry and 13 per cent for municipal uses.
  - The site blocked automated reading, so this wording comes from search results.
- **On-screen source tag:** "SOURCE: FAO AQUASTAT 2025". "About seventy percent" and "≈70%" sit within 71–72%.
