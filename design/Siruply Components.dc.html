How it behaves
The crop always shows the focused field’s region plus about 30% surrounding paper, with the region itself outlined in focus. Context and precision at once — a bare crop loses the line above, which is often what disambiguates the handwriting.
Zoom is per‑field, not fixed: a two‑character quantity gets 2.1×, a full directions line gets 0.57×, so handwriting lands at a readable size regardless of how much was written.
The outline carries a white halo so it stays visible over both ink and bright paper.
Boost is one tap, no submenu, and it is a contrast lift rather than a brightness lift — counter photos are washed out by glare, not dark, so raising brightness makes them worse. Filled button means on.
All labels sit below the crop on solid ground. No text is ever set over the photograph.
The photo here is a placeholder standing in for a counter photo. Drop a real prescription scan in to pressure‑test the crops.

Three options, one treatment
Identical border, identical fill, identical height, identical type. No preselection, no check pre‑placed, no “recommended”, no model name, and no percentage anywhere.
The ordering rule is stated in the row itself, in the last line: numeric values ascend, text values sort alphabetically by the interface locale. Naming the rule is what stops position from being read as rank — without it, the top option is the recommendation whether you intended one or not.
“Ввести своё” is the third option, not a link under them, so entering your own reading costs the same one tap as taking a machine reading.
Cover both value labels and the row gives you no way to guess which came from which reader, or which is preferred. That is the test this component is built to pass.

Nothing written ≠ written illegibly
These two are the pair most likely to be confused and the pair where confusion is most expensive, so they are separated on three axes at once: glyph ∅ against ~, a dashed rail and dashed badge against solid ones, and an empty struck slot where the illegible row has none.

An annotation can never look like a value
Four things keep the two apart, and they are structural rather than stylistic: the annotation is indented past the value’s left edge, sits on shelf rather than paper, is set at body size and weight where values are 20–28px and 600–700, and is below the value it comments on.
It also says so in words: “Справка не подставляет значение” — the note does not fill in the field. The registry is a source of doubt here, never a source of answers.

The eye must land on the ingredient, not the spelling
So the two active ingredients are the largest type in the annotation at 21/700, and the drug names that caused the collision are demoted to 16px mono captions above them. The comparison is inverted on purpose: the confusable strings are small, the thing that actually differs is big.
Two boxes, stacked and separately bordered, never on one line — side by side invites reading them as similar. Stacked with the ingredient names left‑aligned puts амоксициллин directly above ампициллин, where the divergence is impossible to miss.
“Уточнить у врача” is listed first and confirming is second, separated by a 24px gap. This is the one state where the order of the actions is itself a safety decision: the more cautious action is closer to the thumb, and the confirm names the specific drug it commits to rather than saying “OK”.
Still no percentage, and still no ranking. The row raises a doubt and hands it over; it does not resolve it.

Note what is absent: no confirm button, no state label, no glyph. There is nothing to tap, so there is no way to accept it.
A hollow rail that fills in
The rail is drawn as an empty outline while one reading is missing and becomes solid the moment the second lands. An outline reads as an unfilled container, which is exactly the claim: this field is not yet complete. Scanning the rail column alone tells you how much of the form is still arriving.
The value from the fast reading is shown, at full size and full contrast, because reading it is useful work the pharmacist can start immediately. What is withheld is not the information but the ability to act on it.
No skeleton shimmer anywhere in this product. Shimmer is decorative motion that says “something is coming” without saying what or how much, and it habituates instantly — within a week it is wallpaper. A rail that is either hollow or solid cannot be tuned out, because it is the same element that carries state after loading ends.

The button is replaced, not greyed
In a and b there is no Завершить element on screen at all. A greyed‑out button is still a button, and a tired hand taps it anyway to see what happens; a slot that reads “Осталось решений: 2 · Дозировка · Количество” answers the question the tap was going to ask.
a states counts, not progress: seven cells, four filled, and the sentence that says what the cells mean. The cells are the same hollow‑to‑solid language as the field rails, so the bar and the rows agree.
b names the fields that are open, so the remaining work is legible without scrolling, and the dashed slot sits exactly where the enabled button will appear.
c is the only state with a filled button, and it reports the “check with the doctor” count as ordinary summary information rather than a warning. One prescription in the archive with a query on it is a normal day.
No spinner and no progress ring in any of the three. Counts in words tell you whether to wait or to go and get the phone; a ring does not.

The doctor‑query row uses the same type, the same thumbnail, the same ink. It is not dimmed, not badged in warning colour, not sorted to the bottom.
Identity is recorded, so it is on screen
The counter device is shared across a shift, so the chip states who is verifying at all times and switching is a PIN, not a sign‑out. Two taps, and no re‑entry of the branch — the device belongs to the branch, the person does not.
Initials sit on a filled square only for the current user; other staff get outlined squares. Colour alone never marks who is active.
Archive rows carry the active ingredient under the brand name because that is what search runs on. Thumbnail and transcription always travel together: tapping a row opens the photo and the fields on one screen, never one without the other.

Every warning names a place and a fix
“Плохое фото” gives a pharmacist nothing to do. “Блик на левом крае” tells them which way to tilt the phone, and it takes two seconds.
The marginal case gets equal‑weight retake and continue buttons, and it predicts the consequence: this field will probably come back as a manual decision. That lets someone with a customer waiting make an informed trade rather than a blind one.
Warnings appear live over the viewfinder and block nothing except the cropped‑edge case, where part of the prescription is simply not in the frame and no reading is possible.
