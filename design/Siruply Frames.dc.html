Frames
Phone 390×844 and tablet 1024×768 landscape. Russian throughout, with two Azerbaijani Verify frames at the longest plausible string lengths.
Rationale
A phone cannot show a prescription photo and seven fields side by side at a legible size, so this design stops trying. The fields are the whole column and the photo is reduced to a pinned strip above them that only ever shows one thing: the handwriting for the field currently in focus, cropped to that region plus about a third again of surrounding paper and outlined inside the crop. Tapping a row slides the crop; the zoom is computed per field, so a two-character quantity arrives at 2.1× and a full directions line at 0.57×, and the handwriting lands at a readable size either way. The strip is small because it does not need to be big — it is not a document viewer, it is the answer to one question asked seven times. The full sheet is one tap away for the rare case where the surrounding lines matter, and on tablet landscape the strip becomes a real left pane with the whole photo and the same linked outline, because there the room genuinely exists.
Keeping the two readings unranked took removing things rather than balancing them. There are no confidence numbers anywhere in the product, because a number is a ranking and a ranking reads as permission; no model names, because they become a folk hierarchy within a week; no preselected option and no “recommended” marker. The two readings render in one component with identical borders, fills, heights and type, and “enter my own” is the third option in that same stack rather than a link underneath, so disagreeing with both machines costs the same single tap as agreeing with one. That left position itself as the last hidden ranking, and the fix is that the row states its own sort rule in the last line — numeric values ascend, text sorts alphabetically. Naming the rule is what turns the top option from a recommendation into an accident of arithmetic.
A partial result is made unmistakable by withholding capability rather than information. The fast reading’s value appears immediately at full size and full contrast, because reading it is useful work; what does not exist yet is any control that would close the field. Every field carries a left rail drawn as a hollow outline while its second reading is outstanding, filling solid only when it lands, so the rail column alone shows how much of the form has arrived — and because the rail is the same element that carries state afterwards, it cannot be tuned out the way a shimmer is by the end of the first week. There is no skeleton shimmer here at all. The completion control is not disabled during this window; it is not rendered, and in its place sits a plain sentence with counts — «Второе прочтение: 4 из 7 полей», then «Осталось решений: 2» with the open fields named. A greyed button is still a button and a tired hand taps it to see what happens; a sentence that answers the question the tap was going to ask does not need to be defended against.

What the longer strings cost
Azerbaijani runs 15–35% longer than the Russian for the same string, and the pressure lands in three specific places.
Paired action buttons. «Ввести вручную» / «Уточнить у врача» fit on one line each; «Əl ilə daxil et» / «Həkimlə dəqiqləşdir» do not. The pair is built to wrap to two lines and grow rather than shrink the type, which is why every action button is min-height and not height. Nothing truncates.
The look-alike annotation. «təsiredici maddə fərqlidir» pushes the note title to two lines. The ingredient boxes below it are unaffected because they are stacked, not side by side — a two‑column comparison would have broken here.
The strip caption. «7 sahədən 3-cü» is a different grammatical shape from «поле 3 из 7», not just longer, so the caption is one string per locale rather than a template with numbers slotted in.
The three pressure points are split across two frames because the Azerbaijani look-alike card alone is taller than one screen — itself the finding, and the reason the field column scrolls rather than compresses. The Azerbaijani here is a working draft and is marked as such; it needs a native pharmacist’s read before build, particularly the verbs on the resolution actions.
